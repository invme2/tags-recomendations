Three years of refinement

Built around the way you wear it.

We started with one rule: nothing on the shirt should age badly. Then we redesigned every part.

The flax

Grown in the same five fields. Every season.

European flax fibre, harvested by hand and rain-retted in the field. The longest, finest fibres in the world — the only ones we use.

The weave

240 hours on a 1962 loom.

Woven in Kortrijk on a shuttle loom older than every person on the line. Slower, denser, softer. You feel it the first time you put it on.

The finish

Washed eight times before it leaves us.

So the first time you wash it, nothing changes. The second time, it gets a little softer. By the hundredth, it's the shirt you reach for.

Wearing it

Four ways to make it yours.

  1. 01

    Wash it once before you wear it.

    Cold cycle, no detergent. The fibres relax and the shirt drops a half-size.

  2. 02

    Wear it untucked.

    The hem is cut straight for a reason. Layer over a tee, under a jacket, or alone with sleeves rolled to the elbow.

  3. 03

    Don't iron the wrinkles out.

    Linen creases. That's the point. Hang it overnight in the bathroom and the worst will fall out.

  4. 04

    Get it wet.

    Salt water, rain, the lake. Linen is one of the few fabrics that gets stronger when it's wet.

Why it lasts

Flax fibres are hollow and longer than cotton fibres. The hollow core moves moisture away from skin; the length means fewer fibre ends per inch — less pilling, less breakdown over washes.

Fabric performance varies with care. We're a shirt company — not making medical claims.

Before you ask

Questions, answered.

Will it shrink in the wash?
A little. We pre-wash eight times before it leaves us, so the first time you wash it at home, the change is minimal — usually under 2%. After that, it stays put.
What if I'm between sizes?
Size down. The fabric relaxes a half-size after the first wash, and the cut is built to wear loose to start with.
Why does linen wrinkle so much?
Because the fibre is hollow. That's what makes it cooling. The wrinkles are the trade-off — and they fall out the moment you hang it overnight.
Can I tumble dry it?
Yes — on low. We test every shirt against 50 tumble cycles. It softens the hand and shortens dry time. Hang dry works too.
Where exactly is it made?
Flax grown in northern France. Woven in Kortrijk, Belgium. Cut and sewn in Porto, Portugal. Three countries, one shirt.
